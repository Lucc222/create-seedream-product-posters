# Commercial Completeness and Impact

Read this reference before planning any poster whose brief is only “做一个海报,” or when the user asks for a richer, stronger, more impressive, campaign-like result. The default is a complete commercial poster, not a product photo with typography.

## 1. Distinguish a poster from an editorial still

An editorial still can be beautiful with only a product, scenery, headline, and quiet caption. A commercial poster must add a visible campaign idea and a reason to remember the product.

Reject a direction when all of the following are true:

- the background supplies nearly all the beauty;
- the product merely sits, hangs, or floats inside it;
- the proof is only three text labels;
- nothing in the scene visibly reacts to the product;
- removing the copy would leave an ordinary lifestyle photograph;
- the bottom ends with a weak caption rather than a visual or verbal payoff.

Do not confuse emptiness with premium restraint. Premium work still needs a precise visual thesis, material proof, signature memory cue, and intentional close.

## 2. Choose impact mode

### quiet-premium

Use only when the user explicitly asks for minimal, restrained, luxury, gallery, or editorial treatment.

- 6–8 semantic modules;
- at least three impact devices;
- one material proof;
- one composition-tension device;
- one authorized brand or campaign-memory device.

### commercial-rich

Default for an ordinary request to “做一个商品海报.”

- 9–11 semantic modules;
- four or five richness layers including interaction;
- at least four impact devices;
- six information zones: ownership, headline, proof, hero, secondary evidence/story, closing;
- one visual event visible without reading copy.

### campaign-maximal

Use for festivals, IP, social storytelling, high-energy youth campaigns, or when the user explicitly asks for “热闹、炸、牛逼、冲击力强.”

- 11–15 modules grouped into three super-containers;
- all five richness layers;
- at least five impact devices;
- controlled annotation density, not random stickers.

## 3. Build the six completeness components

Every concept must state:

1. **Visual thesis** — the single campaign idea visible at thumbnail size.
2. **Hero event** — what the product causes, interrupts, reveals, transforms, or participates in.
3. **Evidence scene** — a material macro, ingredient world, use action, construction callout, ritual sequence, environmental reaction, or product result.
4. **Brand memory device** — authorized logo/wordmark, brand color block, product-shape symbol, campaign seal, or custom typographic lockup. For unbranded products, use a campaign symbol or signature frame; never invent a brand.
5. **Composition tension** — scale break, asymmetric overlap, edge crop, diagonal, split scene, depth frame, or collage stack.
6. **Bottom payoff** — a visual continuation plus slogan, collection line, product ritual, trust close, or verified offer. Publicity posters remain non-interactive.

If any component can be copied unchanged to an unrelated product, make it more specific.

## 4. Impact-device vocabulary

Select devices from three groups. Every concept needs at least one from each group.

### Composition

- scale-break
- asymmetric-overlap
- diagonal-tension
- edge-crop
- depth-frame
- split-scene
- collage-stack

### Product evidence

- material-macro
- use-action
- ingredient-world
- environmental-reaction
- construction-callout
- ritual-sequence
- product-result

### Memory

- brand-color-block
- logo-halo
- campaign-symbol
- custom-type-lockup
- signature-badge
- signature-border

Do not count generic bokeh, sunlight, leaves, sparkles, lens flare, gradients, or rounded text pills as impact devices.

## 5. Apparel correction

For a floral dress, do not stop at “dress hanging in a garden.”

Build a fashion campaign:

- use an asymmetric 65/35 composition or a diagonal hem sweep;
- make the dress interact with one directional wind path or architectural shadow;
- add one magnified print/fabric inset, contact-sheet crop, or seam/strap detail;
- echo the floral repeat as a restrained graphic field, not more random flowers;
- use one collection eyebrow and one typographic season device;
- close with a non-interactive collection line integrated with the hem or ground plane;
- forbid “AI生成,” fake year codes, fake brand initials, random English, and unlisted microcopy.

The hanger must be plausible and secondary. The product, not the garden, must own the frame.

## 6. Iced-coffee correction

For a branded iced Americano, do not stop at “cup on blue gradient.”

Build a beverage event:

- make the cup 50–65% of poster height and preserve the exact wall, fill line, ice scale, logo, condensation, and label;
- let ice, coffee refraction, condensation, shadow, or a controlled liquid arc create one directed event around the cup without crossing its wall;
- derive a brand-memory device from authorized brand color and visible logo geometry;
- add one secondary evidence scene such as a macro ice window, coffee-light refraction, or a morning ritual strip;
- use type as a spatial shape—cropped, stacked, split, or interacting with the cup—not merely top-aligned;
- close with a non-interactive seasonal line or brand statement when no CTA is requested.

Never invent roast level, caffeine effect, nutrition, sugar/fat status, ingredient, price, volume, store availability, or promotion. “深烘精粹,” “醒神,” and “0糖0脂” require explicit user-supplied evidence.

## 7. Native-generation prompt block

Add this block to every integrated prompt:

~~~text
这不是极简画册封面或带字产品摄影，而是一张完整商业海报。
视觉命题为[visual thesis]；商品必须触发[hero event]。
用[evidence scene]提供可见证据，用[brand memory device]建立记忆，
通过[composition tension]制造构图张力，并以[bottom payoff]完成收口。
画面包含ownership、headline、proof、hero、secondary和closing六个信息区，
但统一在一个视觉系统中。禁止空泛渐变背景、普通景观摆拍、三条文字标签充当全部证明。
~~~

Also forbid:

~~~text
除copy manifest和商品原有标签外，不出现任何文字、字母、年份、编号、水印、
“AI生成”、假品牌缩写、随机英文或伪排版字符。
~~~

## 8. Acceptance questions

Inspect at full size and thumbnail:

1. Without reading, can a viewer state the campaign idea?
2. Does the product cause a visible event?
3. Is there a second evidence/story zone beyond the hero?
4. Is one memory cue specific to the authorized brand, campaign, or product geometry?
5. Does the composition contain tension rather than centered placement alone?
6. Does the bottom pay off the concept?
7. Would the image still look like a commercial poster if all benefit text were removed?

Any “no” is a revision. If question 7 is “no,” regenerate rather than polishing the typography.
